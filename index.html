Featured Project: CMS Medicare Inpatient FFS Analytics (2019–2023)

An end-to-end Medicare claims analytics project built on ~790K provider-DRG records spanning 5 years. Covers descriptive analysis, multi-year trend modeling, CAH vs. non-CAH reimbursement dynamics, statistical process control, outlier detection, and ML anomaly detection with applied AI governance analysis. Built in Python (Pandas, Matplotlib, Scikit-learn, SHAP) with a PostgreSQL backend.

1. Descriptive Analysis — 2023 Snapshot

Baseline analysis of 146K+ provider-DRG combinations. Key findings: hospitals bill 6.34x what Medicare pays on average; Nevada is a persistent high outlier; urban providers receive $4,300 more per case than rural; septicemia dominates discharge volume.

Tech: Python, Pandas, Matplotlib, Seaborn, PostgreSQL

2. Multi-Year Trend Analysis (2019–2023)

Tracked discharge volumes (−29% over 5 years), average Medicare payments (+18%), and charge-to-payment ratio trends post-COVID. Demonstrated that the rural/urban payment gap widened over time, with urban providers averaging $15.3K vs. rural at $11.5K per case by 2023.

Tech: Python, Pandas, Matplotlib, PostgreSQL

3. CAH vs. Non-CAH Provider Analysis

Joined the CMS Provider of Services file to segment rural providers by CAH status. Counterintuitive finding: CAHs show lower Medicare payments AND lower charge-to-payment ratios than rural non-CAH hospitals, consistent with cost-based reimbursement working as intended. Financial risk concentrates in rural non-CAH facilities caught between fixed DRG rates and high-acuity patients.

Tech: Python, Pandas, Matplotlib, Seaborn, PostgreSQL

4. Statistical Process Control & Funnel Plot Analysis

Applied multi-year control charts to detect state-level billing outliers, then identified SPC assumption violations (uneven sample sizes, structural heterogeneity). Rebuilt using funnel plots, which corrected Nevada's false-positive outlier status and identified Florida as a persistent high outlier across all 5 years. Maryland's consistent low outlier status traced to its all-payer rate-setting system — a natural experiment in alternative payment models.

Tech: Python, Pandas, Matplotlib, SciPy

AI Governance

5. Isolation Forest Anomaly Detection — Peer Group Modeling & SHAP Explainability

Trained an Isolation Forest model on 790K+ Medicare claims. Initial single-model approach flagged rural providers at 33.5% and CAH providers at 58.9% — vs. 3.7% for urban — revealing structural bias from training on an urban-majority dataset. Rebuilt as three peer-group models (CAH, rural non-CAH, urban/suburban), each learning "normal" within its own structural context — mirroring CMS's own provider-profiling methodology.

SHAP analysis revealed distinct anomaly drivers by group: geography dominates for CAHs; volume and data year (post-COVID billing shifts) dominate for rural non-CAH; commercial payer mix dominates for urban/suburban. Demonstrates that deploying the single model in a payment integrity context would disproportionately audit rural and CAH providers — not due to wrongdoing, but due to structural mismatch between billing patterns and model training data.

Tech: Python, Scikit-learn (Isolation Forest), SHAP, Pandas, Matplotlib, PostgreSQL
